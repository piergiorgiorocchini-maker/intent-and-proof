### Integration by levels

A practice does not need to begin with a complete bidirectional API. A pragmatic roadmap has three levels.

**Level 1: controlled manual process.** The front desk updates stages in a simple CRM or structured spreadsheet. This is sufficient to validate the taxonomy and discover which data are genuinely used.

**Level 2: import and export.** Scheduled CSV files or no-code connectors transfer enquiries and stages. This is less elegant than an API but allows the model to be tested at lower cost.

**Level 3: API connectors.** The gateway creates or updates records and receives events. This level requires documentation, authentication, logging, error handling and agreements with vendors.

Starting at level 3 before the stages have been defined creates an expensive integration that automates confusion.

### APIs, webhooks and polling

A webhook is useful when the practice-management system can notify a change, such as completion of a first visit. Polling is needed where notifications do not exist: the system checks periodically for updated stages. Manual import remains an option when the vendor exposes no suitable interface.

Every method requires idempotency, meaning the ability to avoid duplication. The same event received twice must not create two first visits or two values.

The system should also record:

- time of transmission;
- response from the practice system;
- any error;
- number of attempts;
- payload version;
- user or process responsible.

These logs are essential when reports do not agree.

### Marketing should not choose the practice-management system

Compatibility with the acquisition infrastructure is an important criterion, but it cannot replace clinical, administrative and legal requirements. Intent & Proof should not recommend changing practice software merely because one connector is easier to build.

The correct strategy is to adapt to the systems genuinely used by early clients, document limitations and build a common integration layer. Over time, the most requested connectors become product assets.

### A shared taxonomy

Every practice-management system uses different names. One may say “new patient”, another “first visit”, another “accepted estimate”. The integration layer should translate these stages into a common taxonomy.

An initial model may include:

**lead_created → contact_attempted → contact_reached → appointment_booked → appointment_confirmed → first_visit_completed → internal_outcome_recorded**

Clinical stages remain outside the marketing model. The taxonomy should be precise enough to support decisions and simple enough to be used every day.

## Closed-loop attribution: from click to first visit without sending health data to advertising platforms

The most powerful idea in the architecture is connecting the source of an enquiry to later outcomes. It is also the point at which a technically impressive project can become unlawful or breach platform policy by transferring the wrong data.

The conceptual flow is:

```text
Google Ads / local SEO
        ↓
Landing page with attribution parameters
        ↓
Lead Gateway
        ↓
Practice CRM or management software
        ↓
Contact, appointment and first visit
        ↓
Data-minimised internal dashboard
        ↓
Decisions about budget, content and process
```

The dashboard may show that a campaign generated forty enquiries, ten reached contacts, three first visits and a given internal economic value. That information is enough to discuss responsibility and budget allocation.

It does not mean the same value can be sent to Google.

### The most important correction: enhanced conversions cannot measure sensitive healthcare services

Google supports offline conversion imports and, in many industries, enhanced conversions for leads. Enhanced conversions use user-provided information, such as hashed email addresses or telephone numbers, to improve matching between the original lead and a later offline event. From 15 June 2026, Google directs new upload implementations toward the Data Manager API. [Source: Google Ads, offline conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7012522?hl=en)

However, Google's customer-data policies state that **conversions relating to sensitive categories cannot be used for measurement with enhanced conversions**. Google's examples include medical information and purchases of healthcare services. [Source: Google Ads, Customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709?hl=en)

This changes the idea of sending Google an event such as “treatment plan accepted” with a value of €4,500 and patient identifiers. That flow should not be presented as a standard feature of a dental product.

The internal dashboard may contain the value under the appropriate legal basis, roles and safeguards. The advertising platform should not automatically receive a healthcare or economic outcome linked to an identifiable patient.

### Standard offline conversion imports and areas that require review

Google still documents offline imports based on GCLID and advertiser-defined conversion actions. The policies on enhanced conversions are explicit; the use of standard imports in healthcare nevertheless requires specific review of the applicable policy, the data being transmitted and the legal basis.
